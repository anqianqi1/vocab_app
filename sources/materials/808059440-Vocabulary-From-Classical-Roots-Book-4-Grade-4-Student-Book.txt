       Preface
              Vocabulary from Classical Roots ®encourages you to look at words as members of families. Just
              as members of a human family usually have things in common, so do members of word
              families. In this book you will get to know groups of words descended from Latin and
              Greek roots. Each word family has a root that is connected to a concept, or idea. The idea
              of “serving and helping,” for example, is rooted in serv from the Latin word servire. It is easy
              to see that such words as service, server, and serviceable are members of the serv family.

              Latin and Greek roots are word parts that carry meaning. Learning these roots will help
              you understand many words that contain these meaningful parts. If you know that the
              Latin root vari means “different, varied,” then you know part of the meaning of various,
              variety, and variation. Not only does the knowledge of classical roots increase your vocabu-
              lary. It also shows you that many words you use today, such as technology and classification,
              have ancestry that can be traced back for centuries.


       Notes on Using Vocabulary from Classical Roots
              1. Latin and Greek forms. The Latin or Greek source of each root appears with its meaning
              before the definitions of its featured words.

              2. Diacritical marks. Following every defined word is the guide to pronunciation. You will
              find a key to the diacritical marks on the inside front cover.

              3. Familiar Words and Challenge Words. Listed next to groups of defined words are two
              boxes of words belonging to the same family: (1) Familiar Words that you probably already
              know, and (2) Challenge Words that you may want to figure out.

              4. Nota Bene. Nota bene means “note well.” It is often abbreviated to N.B. Each NOTA
              BENE will give you some extra information about a key word.

              5. Exercises. In each lesson, Exercise A focuses on synonyms. Exercise B asks you to use key
              words to fill in blanks in context. Exercise C features a suffix related to a key word. The
              review lessons reinforce meanings of roots, prefixes, suffixes, and key words.

                  EXERCISE C: Extend Your Vocabulary
                  The adjective suffix -less
                  The key word motionless ends in the suffix -less. Words ending in -less are usually
                  adjectives. Adjectives describe people, places, and things. Examples: helpless babies,
                  cloudless skies, useless junk. When the suffix -less is added to a word, the meaning of
                  the word changes.

                  Less can also stand alone. Then, it is a word meaning “fewer; a smaller amount.”
                  You can say, “After dogs shed, they have less hair,” or “If you would talk less, you
                  might learn more.” In these sentences, less is a word, not a suffix.
